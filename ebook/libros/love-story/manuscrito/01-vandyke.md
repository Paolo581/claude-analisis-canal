# Part One: The Second Chance

## Chapter 1. Dick Van Dyke: Married at 86

In 2006, at an awards dinner in Los Angeles, an eighty-year-old Dick Van Dyke went looking for someone to fix his makeup and found a thirty-four-year-old makeup artist named Arlene Silver. They talked. He thought she was funny. She thought he was kind. He asked if she would like to work with him on a few projects. She said yes.

Nothing happened for years. Then, slowly, something did.

By the time they married, in February 2012, he was eighty-six and she was forty. He had been a widower for three years, after the death of Michelle Triola, the woman he had lived with for thirty-five years. Before that, he had been married for thirty-six years to Margie Willett, the mother of his four children. He was, by any measure, a man at the end of a long romantic life, not the beginning of one.

The newspapers did the math and had a field day. Forty-six years. His children were older than his bride. The internet assumed the worst.

What did Dick Van Dyke say? He said, cheerfully, that he had no idea why she married him and he was not going to question it. He said that his children had met her, spent time with her, and liked her. He said that she made him laugh every single day. And he said something that every person over sixty-five who is thinking about love should hear: "I think people my age are sometimes afraid to start over. I wasn't. What was I waiting for?"

Then he went and lived another fourteen years, and counting.

Here is what the marriage looks like from the inside, by their own accounts. They sing together; she is in his barbershop group. They go to the gym together three mornings a week, which is why he is still walking. She films him dancing in the kitchen. They have made a short film together. She has said that she never thinks about the age difference except when a reporter brings it up. He has said that she is the reason he gets up in the morning, and that he thinks he will live to a hundred because of her. He turned a hundred in December 2025. He was right.

### What this means for you

Dick Van Dyke's story is the extreme case, which is why it is useful. If a man of eighty-six can start over, so can a woman of seventy. Here is what his story actually teaches, apart from the headline.

**Late love usually begins as friendship.** They worked together for years before anything else. Older couples who last almost always describe the same thing: a slow start, built on company and laughter, long before romance. You are not looking for a date. You are looking for someone you like to talk to. The rest follows or it doesn't.

**Your children will have opinions. Listen, then decide.** Van Dyke's children met Arlene early, often, and on their own terms, and he did not ask their permission but he did want their blessing. Adult children worry about two things: that you will be hurt, and, quietly, about money. Both are fair. Introduce the person early. Be open about your finances and your will. And remember that your children do not get a vote on your happiness, only a voice.

**Age gaps are nobody's business but yours, with one caution.** A large gap is fine if you have asked yourself honestly why this person is with you, and if your money is protected. More on that in Chapter 4 and at the back of the book.

**The things that keep you alive are the things you do together.** The gym three mornings a week. The singing. The laughing. Married older adults live longer than single ones, and the research is clear on why: someone to notice when you are unwell, someone to eat with, someone to get you out of the chair. You do not need a spouse for that. You need a person. But a spouse counts.

**"What was I waiting for?"** Most women over sixty-five who say they are done with love have not decided that. They have assumed it. Van Dyke's question is the one to ask.

### Three things to do this week

1. **Answer the question honestly.** On paper: "Would I like someone in my life again? Yes, no, or not sure." There is no wrong answer. But know yours.
2. **Name the fear.** If the answer is yes but you have done nothing, write down what stops you: your children, your body, your money, your late husband, looking foolish. Every one of those has a chapter in this book.
3. **Say yes to one invitation** you would normally decline, from anyone, for any reason. Dick Van Dyke went to an awards dinner he did not much feel like attending.
