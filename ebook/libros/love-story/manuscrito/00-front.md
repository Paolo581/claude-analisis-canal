## Welcome

Dick Van Dyke married the love of his life at eighty-six.

Joan Collins married hers at sixty-eight, and when people pointed out that he was thirty-two years younger, she said, "If he dies, he dies."

Carol Burnett married at sixty-eight too, a man she met in an orchestra pit, and has spent twenty-four years with him.

Betty White was widowed at fifty-nine, never remarried, and lived forty more years so full of friends, work and laughter that nobody ever thought to call her alone.

Dolly Parton was married to the same man for fifty-eight years, and buried him in the spring of 2025, and is still, at eighty, finding a way through.

And Diane Keaton never married at all, by choice, and was, by every account including her own, one of the happiest women in Hollywood.

Every week on our channel we open the files on the private lives of the stars, and nothing brings more of you to the comments than love: the marriages that lasted, the ones that broke, the second chances, the losses. But under those comments there is almost always a quieter question. *What about me?*

What about me, widowed at seventy-one, when everyone assumes that part of my life is over? What about me, divorced at sixty-six, who hasn't been on a date since Nixon? What about me, married forty years to a man who is now home all day and driving me up the wall? What about me, whose friends are dying, whose children live three states away, who has not been hugged in a month?

This book is the answer, and the stars are the proof.

It is in five parts. **The Second Chance**: how the stars who found love late actually did it, and the practical guide to dating after sixty-five, from the first phone call to the prenup. **The Long Love**: what the fifty-year marriages have in common, and how to survive retirement, illness and the quiet years together. **After Loss**: what the stars did in the first year of widowhood, and what the research says protects you. **Friends and Family**: the truth about the Golden Girls, how to make a friend at seventy, and how to be the grandmother they call. And **Yourself**: the stars who lived alone and loved it, and how to build a life that is full whether or not anyone else is in it.

Every chapter ends with three things you can do this week. Some are as small as a phone call. All of them are things a star in this book actually did.

Love after sixty-five is not a consolation prize. Ask Dick Van Dyke.

*The Behind the Fame team*
