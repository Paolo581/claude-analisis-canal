## Chapter 11. The four-week menu

Four weeks of the Hollywood plate, built from the recipes in Part One. The portions are for one; double everything for two. Each day gives you about 1,400 to 1,600 calories and 75 to 90 grams of protein, which lets most women over sixty-five lose weight slowly while keeping their strength. If your doctor wants you at a different number, add or remove the starch and the afternoon snack first.

**Every day, whatever the menu says:** a glass of water with each meal and one between. Coffee or tea without sugar. A piece of fruit and a small handful of nuts in the afternoon. One square of dark chocolate after dinner, then the kitchen is closed.

**Every Sunday:** make Betty's chicken soup or Audrey's vegetable soup for the freezer, Martha's roasted vegetable tray, Julia's vinaigrette, and a dozen boiled eggs. That one hour makes the week easy.

### Week 4: Your week

By now you have twenty-six recipes and you know which ones you like. Build this week yourself with the five rules:

- **Breakfast:** eggs or Greek yogurt, every day, with fruit or a slice of toast.
- **Lunch:** a soup from the freezer, a big salad with protein, or the cottage cheese plate.
- **Dinner:** half vegetables, a palm of protein, a small starch. Fish twice this week. Beans twice. Chicken twice. One night of whatever you love, in Dolly's spoonfuls.
- **Sunday:** cook the pot for next week.

Write it on the refrigerator on Saturday. Shop once. A written menu is the difference between the Hollywood plate and the toast-and-tea plate, and it takes ten minutes.

## Chapter 12. Cooking for one, and the pantry that makes it easy

Most of the stars in this book cooked, or had cooked for them, in large kitchens for large tables. You may be cooking for one, in a small kitchen, with a bad knee. Here is how to make the menu work anyway.

### Eight tricks for cooking for one

1. **Cook once, eat three times.** Every soup, the roast chicken, the sheet pan and the roasted tray are built to be eaten across the week. Cook on Sunday, portion into containers, and the weekday meal is three minutes in the microwave.
2. **Freeze in single portions.** Soup in one-cup containers or freezer bags laid flat. Cooked chicken in half-cup bags. Label with the date. Three months is fine.
3. **Boil a dozen eggs on Sunday.** They keep a week in the shell in the refrigerator and solve breakfast, lunch, and every salad.
4. **Buy frozen vegetables without shame.** They are picked ripe, cost less, and never go soft in the drawer. Keep spinach, green beans, broccoli, peas and a stir-fry mix in the freezer always.
5. **Buy canned fish and beans by the case.** Tuna, salmon, sardines, white beans, chickpeas, pintos, lentils. A can of fish and a can of beans is a meal with 40 grams of protein and no cooking.
6. **A small nonstick skillet, a medium pot, and a sheet pan** are the only equipment this book needs. If your hands hurt, a small food processor for chopping and an electric can opener are worth every dollar.
7. **Set the table.** Even for one. A plate, a glass, a napkin, the radio. Sophia and Audrey never ate standing at the counter, and neither should you. Sitting down is how you eat slowly, and eating slowly is how you eat less.
8. **If you cannot cook some days, do not skip.** Greek yogurt and fruit. Cottage cheese and tomato. Sardines on toast. A boiled egg and cheese and an apple. All of them are a meal with protein, and all take two minutes.

With these in the house, you can make most of this book without a shopping trip.

**Shelf:** olive oil, canned tomatoes, canned beans, canned tuna and salmon and sardines, red lentils, small pasta and spaghetti, brown rice, oats, low-sodium broth, dried herbs (oregano, thyme, rosemary, Italian seasoning), cumin, paprika, garlic, onions, potatoes, sweet potatoes, nuts, dark chocolate, honey, vinegar, Dijon mustard, low-sodium soy sauce.

**Freezer:** spinach, green beans, broccoli, peas, stir-fry mix, berries, chicken thighs, salmon or white fish fillets, shrimp, bread, and your own soups.

### If you have a grandchild in the kitchen

Give them the cornbread, the banana pudding, the roast chicken to baste, and the omelet to flip. Every star in this book learned to eat from a grandmother. You are the grandmother now.
