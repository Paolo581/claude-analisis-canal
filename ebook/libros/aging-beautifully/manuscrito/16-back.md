## The Stars' Morning

Here is what happens if you put the stars' habits together into one ordinary morning. You do not need all of it. Pick three lines and do them for a month.

| Time | Habit | Who taught us |
|---|---|---|
| On waking | Thirty quiet minutes before the world: prayer, a book, the window, five slow breaths. | Dolly Parton |
| Then | A glass of water. Your thirst is quieter than it used to be. | Sophia Loren |
| Then | Twelve minutes beside the bed: march, reach, sit-to-stand, heel raises, balance. | Helen Mirren |
| Then | Two minutes with your face in the morning light. | Goldie Hawn |
| Breakfast | Something with protein: eggs, yogurt, cheese, nuts. A handful of greens if you can. | Jane Fonda, Martha Stewart |
| With coffee | One crossword or puzzle, in ink. | Betty White, Olivia de Havilland |
| Before going out | Moisturizer on face and hands. Hat on. Shoes with a back and a grip. | Joan Collins, Iris Apfel |
| Mid-morning | A walk. Twenty minutes, with a friend, a dog, or a song. | Dick Van Dyke |
| All day | Stand up every half hour. Dance to one song. Laugh at something. | Betty White |
| Evening | Dinner with olive oil, vegetables, beans or fish. Stop when satisfied. | Sophia Loren |
| Before bed | No screens for the last hour. Go to bed friends. Aim for eight hours. | Goldie Hawn, Sophia Loren |

## Your gentle 30-Day Plan

One small change at a time. Each week adds one habit and keeps the ones before. If you miss a day, you have not failed. Helen Mirren has been missing days since 1965.

**Week 1: Move**

- Day 1. Do the sit-to-stand test. Write your number down.
- Day 2. Put a sturdy chair beside your bed. Do the twelve minutes, or as much as you can.
- Day 3. Start the "stand up every commercial" rule.
- Day 4. Twelve minutes again.
- Day 5. Walk for ten minutes outside, hat on.
- Day 6. Twelve minutes. Dance to one song.
- Day 7. Rest. Call a friend and tell her what you did this week.

**Week 2: Eat**

- Day 8. Put olive oil by the stove. Use it instead of butter today.
- Day 9. Add protein to breakfast. Keep the twelve minutes going three days this week.
- Day 10. A handful of spinach into something.
- Day 11. Read the labels on three foods. Swap one sugary one.
- Day 12. A glass of water with every meal and one between.
- Day 13. At one meal, stop halfway and ask whether you are still hungry.
- Day 14. Rest. Plant something in a pot.

**Week 3: Protect**

- Day 15. Put the moisturizer next to your toothbrush.
- Day 16. Line up your shoes. Retire the dangerous ones.
- Day 17. Write the FAST card and put it on the refrigerator.
- Day 18. Have your blood pressure checked at a pharmacy. Write it down.
- Day 19. Do the wavy-line eye test with each eye.
- Day 20. Book the appointments you have been putting off: skin, eyes, hearing, bone density.
- Day 21. Rest. Learn ten words in a new language.

**Week 4: Connect**

- Day 22. Five minutes of quiet breathing in a chair.
- Day 23. Call the oldest friend you have.
- Day 24. Five minutes of breathing. Finish the sentence: "Tomorrow, someone needs me to..."
- Day 25. Find one weekly thing to show up to: a class, a group, a walk, a service. Write down when it meets.
- Day 26. Write for fifteen minutes about something you have never said.
- Day 27. Get dressed, jewelry on, and do one interesting thing.
- Day 28. Go to the weekly thing.

**Days 29 and 30**

- Day 29. Do the sit-to-stand test again. Compare your number.
- Day 30. Write down the thing you always said was too late. Take the first step.

## A note on talking to your doctor

Several chapters in this book end with a question to ask your doctor. Here they are in one place, so you can take the page with you.

- Should I be screened for osteoporosis? Is strength training safe for me?
- What is my blood pressure, and is it where it should be?
- Have you ever checked me for atrial fibrillation?
- Could any of my medications be making me dizzy, tired, or forgetful?
- I have lost weight without trying. Should we look into that?
- I have been feeling down or not interested in things. Can we talk about that?
- Could I have sleep apnea?
- Is it time for a full skin check, an eye exam, and a hearing test?
- Is the Medicare Annual Wellness Visit due? It is free, and it includes a check on memory, falls, and mood.

Medicare's Annual Wellness Visit costs nothing and is the right place for every question on this list. If you have not had one in the last twelve months, that is the first phone call.

## Numbers to keep by the phone

- **911** for stroke signs, chest pain, or any emergency.
- **988** the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline, call or text, any hour, for yourself or someone you love.
- **1-800-677-1116** the Eldercare Locator, to find your Area Agency on Aging, senior centers, exercise classes, meals, and help at home.
- **1-800-QUIT-NOW** (1-800-784-8669) free help to stop smoking.
- **1-800-MEDICARE** (1-800-633-4227) questions about what Medicare covers.
- **1-800-272-3900** the Alzheimer's Association helpline, 24 hours, for questions about memory, for yourself or a loved one.
