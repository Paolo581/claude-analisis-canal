Where the years go

Three places women over 65 quietly lose their strength

The sofa

After 60, an inactive woman loses muscle every year she sits still. By 70, a third of it can be gone. That is why a jar lid becomes impossible and a stumble becomes a fall. Dick Van Dyke, Helen Mirren and Jane Fonda show how to get it back at home, in twelve minutes, with two cans of soup.

The beauty counter and the vitamin aisle

Creams, serums, "anti-aging" pills. Most of it does nothing. Joan Collins' skin at 92 comes from one habit she has kept since the 1950s, and it costs less than a hat. Because it is a hat.

The quiet house

Loneliness and sitting alone with the television are now counted among the biggest health risks after 65, ahead of most diets. Betty White, Rita Moreno and Dolly Parton each found the same cure, and it was not a pill.

One in four Americans over 65 falls every year. Falls are the leading cause of injury in that age group, and most of them are preventable with the strength and balance habits in Part One. No gym. No membership. A sturdy chair.
