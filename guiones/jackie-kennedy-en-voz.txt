July sixteenth, nineteen ninety-nine. Her son, John Junior, was flying a small plane to the island of Martha's Vineyard, with his wife, Carolyn, and her sister, Lauren, on board. The plane went down in the ocean.

All three died. John was thirty-eight. The little boy who, at three, saluted his father's casket died thirty-five years after that photograph.

Of Jackie's four children, only Caroline was left. And many years later, it was Caroline who had to face a pain much like her mother's.

Caroline's daughter, Tatiana Schlossberg, was a journalist who wrote about the environment. In May of twenty twenty-four, shortly after having her second child, she was diagnosed with leukemia.

In November of twenty twenty-five, Tatiana told her own story in an essay for an American magazine. And she revealed that the disease was terminal.

Tatiana died on December thirtieth, twenty twenty-five, at thirty-five. Jackie's granddaughter left behind a husband and two small children.

In February of twenty twenty-six, the story of John Junior and Carolyn came to American television in a dramatic series. A new generation got to know Jackie's family. And to look again at the woman who stood at the center of it all.

And all the while, in a government building, in a dark room with the temperature carefully controlled, the pink suit from that day in Dallas is still there. Waiting for the year twenty-one oh three.

Looking back, the life of Jacqueline Kennedy seems made of two stories running side by side. One is the story the world saw. The elegance, the restored White House, Paris at her feet, the widow in the black veil who moved the planet.

The other is the one she kept to herself. The father who drank. The husband who cheated. The babies she buried. The days she thought she couldn't go on.

The girl who learned to smile for the cameras while her parents' marriage fell apart became the woman who smiled for the world while her own came apart in exactly the same way.

Her whole life, Jackie chose to show only the first story. And it was behind the most famous smile in the world that she hid the second.

She made sure herself that the image of Camelot would survive. But the full truth about the woman behind that smile only came out after she was gone. In a priest's notes, and in the books of the people who knew her. And one last piece is still missing. A pink suit the world will wait almost eighty years to see.
