### The failed pivot

Since 29 October the channel has been copying the "X's House Was Much NASTIER Than You Thought" and "Stars DESTROYED By Drugs" title style from channels like Whisperland Fables and Famous Yesterday, in 13 to 20 minute videos. Those 12 videos average about 300 views each. Its own formula ("The Final Homes Of Sitcom Stars", same week) did 102 K. Lesson: the audience came for the property-plus-legend list, not for single-person gossip.

### What the hit list tells you about the viewer

The cohorts that win are the ones a 60-year-old American either feared (drug lords, mafia, murder houses) or grew up with on TV and radio (child stars, sitcom casts, country and classic music legends, Golden Age Hollywood), plus the schadenfreude cohort (lottery winners). The cohorts that lose are pre-1920 history (Gilded Age, Texas oil), foreign royals, and niche fandoms (gospel, magicians, comedians, 27 Club). Keep this cohort list. It transfers to any new object you build a channel around.

### Why the format itself works

1. **Two hooks stacked**: a face the viewer already knows, plus a thing they can "walk through" (the house), plus a payoff with a number ("sold for $12 million", "demolished in 2019", "still empty"). Every segment is a 3 to 5 minute mini-story with its own resolution, so a 35 to 85 minute video keeps retention.
2. **Listicle inside a documentary wrapper**: long watch time and many mid-roll slots, which is where the RPM of $6 to $8.5 on a 65+ US audience comes from.
3. **Zero filming cost**: Street View, Zillow and Realtor listing photos, newspaper archive photos, a timeline graphic and a price overlay. Research and script are the real work.
4. **Interchangeable title template**: "[Abandoned | Final | Forgotten] [Homes | Mansions] Of [cohort]". Ten videos a batch, each a cohort swap.

## 5. Channel ideas, ranked

Ranking criteria: same 55+ US audience, demand already visible in NexLev outliers, how many serious channels exist, how cheap the visuals are to source, and estimated RPM.

### 1. "Who inherited the fortune": wills, estates and lost fortunes of legends (top pick)

**Concept.** One legend per video or a list of 8 to 12: what they were worth at death, what the will said, who got the house, the cars, the royalties, who got nothing, the court fights, what the estate is worth today. Object = the money, not the mansion.

**Evidence of demand (all from channels under 10 K subs, last 90 days).**
- "Elvis's Granddaughter Just Inherited His $700M Empire" 257 K (channel of 1.7 K subs, 441x its average)
- "Dolly Parton's Family STUNNED As Lawyers CONFIRM Who Inherits" 573 K
- "Kobe Bryant's Mother Just Inherited $600M" 200 K
- "Prince Philip Left EVERYTHING He Loved To Lady Louise" 434 K
- "How Whitney Houston Lost Her $250 Million Fortune" 95 K, "How Lisa Marie Presley Lost Elvis's $100 Million Fortune" 85 K (Fallen Stars)
- "Edith Rockefeller, The Tragic Fate of Her 5 Children" 989 K (Gilded Fate, RPM 9)

**Competition.** Fallen Stars is the only serious documentary channel and it is three months old. Everything else is clickbait "BREAKING" news channels with fabricated claims, which is exactly the gap: a channel that reads the probate filings and says what is documented.

**Why it fits you.** It is the same viewer and the same single-legend research you already do for Alvina Ericyan, with a money spine instead of a tragedy spine. RPM in this cluster is $7.8 to $9.4 (Fortune's Grave 9.4, Dark Heirs 9, Gilded Fate 9).

**Sample titles.**
- The Will Of Johnny Cash: Who Got The Lake House, The Royalties, And Who Got Nothing
- 12 Country Legends Whose Families Went To War Over The Estate
- What Happened To Elvis's $700 Million After Lisa Marie Died
- The Sitcom Stars Who Died Broke, And Who Paid For The Funeral
- Prince Died Without A Will: Where The $156 Million Went

**Risk.** Legal accuracy. Use court records and reputable press, and say when the record stops. Avoid living-person inheritance speculation.

## 6. How to execute any of the five, copied from what made The Greatest Homes work

1. **Cohort before object.** Use the proven cohort list: drug lords and mafia, child stars, sitcom casts, country legends, classic music legends, Golden Age Hollywood, lottery winners, murdered celebrities. Skip Gilded Age, foreign royals, gospel, magicians, comedians.
2. **Length.** The hits run 20 to 85 minutes. The 20-minute "Sitcom Stars" did 102 K, so 20 to 35 minutes is the efficient zone; go longer only for proven cohorts.
3. **Structure.** 8 to 12 segments, each with: who they were (30 seconds), the object and its price when bought, what happened, what it is today with a Street View or current photo, and the number payoff. Open the video on a specific address or figure, and at 0:30 plant one "here's the thing almost everybody gets wrong" line.
4. **Visuals.** Archival photos, listing photos, Street View, one timeline bar, big price overlays, animated map. No AI imagery: the audience is 65+ and the top channels in this cluster are flagged "not AI content".
5. **Voice.** Human or indistinguishable. The channel's narrator is a calm human male; the female-skewing variants (sitcoms, Golden Girls, country) could use a warm female voice, which also fits Alvina's brand.
6. **Cadence.** The channel reached 1.13 M views in a month with roughly 10 videos a week in bursts. Plan 20 to 30 videos before launch and release 4 to 7 a week for the first six weeks, because the clone window is about eight weeks.
7. **Monetization expectation.** At RPM $6 to $9, 500 K monthly views is $3,000 to $4,500 a month from AdSense alone, which is where The Greatest Homes sat in September and October.
